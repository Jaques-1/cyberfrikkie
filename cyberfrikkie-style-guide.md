# Cyberfrikkie — Brand Style Guide

## 1. Brand essence

**What it is:** A tech blog / personal brand that explains AI, gadgets and digital culture in plain, funny, South African-flavoured language — for people who want to actually understand tech, not just survive the jargon.

**Positioning line:** The smart friend at the braai who happens to know how AI works.

**Personality:** Dry, warm, unpretentious, plain-spoken, quietly sharp. Confident without ever talking down.

**Tagline options:**
- Tech, translated for the rest of us
- Cyber for ordinary ouens
- No jargon. Just Frikkie.

**What the brand is not:** Corporate, hyped-up, jargon-heavy, self-serious, aspirational-lifestyle tech content.

---

## 2. Logo & mark

The wordmark splits the name into its two halves so the joke is visible at a glance:
- **"cyber"** set in cyber green, monospace
- **"frikkie"** set in rust orange, monospace

A subtle double-exposure/glitch offset (a faint duplicate of the wordmark shifted a few pixels) reinforces the CRT/terminal feel. Use this treatment for the primary logo lockup on dark backgrounds only.

**Icon mark:** a circular "braai tongs meets circuit trace" symbol — two crossed lines inside a ring with a single glowing node at the centre. Works as a favicon, social avatar, or standalone mark when the full wordmark won't fit.

**Minimum clear space:** leave space equal to the height of the "c" in "cyber" on all sides of the mark.

**Don't:**
- Don't recolor "cyber" and "frikkie" the same color — the split colour *is* the logo's meaning.
- Don't place the wordmark on busy photography without a dark scrim behind it.
- Don't stretch, rotate, or add drop shadows to the mark.

## 3. Color palette

| Swatch | Hex | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⬛ | `#14100D` | Near black | Primary background |
| 🟩 | `#39FF8A` | Cyber green | Primary accent — links, the "cyber" half of the logo, terminal accents |
| 🟧 | `#E8672C` | Frikkie rust | Secondary accent — the "frikkie" half of the logo, CTAs, highlights |
| ⬜ | `#E8E2D4` | Off-white | Primary text on dark backgrounds |
| ◻️ | `#4A453B` | Khaki grey | Borders, dividers, muted text |

**Usage ratio:** the page should read as 80% near-black + off-white, with green and rust used sparingly as accents — never as large background fills. If everything glows, nothing does.

**Contrast rule:** never place cyber green and frikkie rust directly adjacent at full saturation (they vibrate against each other) — separate them with black, grey, or off-white space.

---

## 4. Typography

**Display / headlines:** a monospace face (e.g. JetBrains Mono, Space Mono, or IBM Plex Mono) — carries the "terminal" personality. Bold weight only, sentence case (never all-caps).

**Body copy:** a warm serif (e.g. Source Serif 4, Lora, or Georgia) — keeps long-form reading comfortable and gives the brand its "human" counterweight to the cold monospace headlines. Line-height 1.6–1.7, line length under 75 characters.

**Small labels / metadata (dates, categories):** the same monospace as headlines, at a smaller size, in khaki grey.

**Type scale (desktop):**
- H1 / hero: 56–64px, monospace, bold
- H2 / section: 32px, monospace, bold
- H3 / card title: 20px, monospace, bold
- Body: 18px, serif, regular
- Small / meta: 13px, monospace, regular, letter-spacing 0.5px

---

## 5. Visual texture & motifs

- **Scanlines:** faint horizontal lines (2–4% opacity) over dark hero sections — used once per page, not layered everywhere.
- **Glitch offset:** a duplicated text layer shifted 2–3px with reduced opacity, used only on the main logo/hero headline — a single deliberate moment, not a page-wide effect.
- **Dashed rules:** thin khaki dashed lines to separate list items or article metadata, evoking a terminal/code aesthetic without being literal ASCII art.
- **No gradients, no neon glow blur, no stock cyberpunk imagery** (neon-soaked cityscapes, katanas, holograms) — the visual restraint is what keeps this from looking like generic "cyberpunk" clip art. The two accent colors and monospace/serif pairing do the work.

---

## 6. Voice & tone in copy

- Plain language first; jargon only when explained in the same sentence.
- Short sentences. Contractions always ("it's", "you'll").
- SA turns of phrase used naturally, not forced into every sentence ("ja nee", "lekker", "eish", "boet") — one per piece, if it fits.
- Self-deprecating rather than boastful; admits uncertainty rather than overclaiming expertise.
- Headlines are plain statements or questions, never clickbait ALL CAPS or listicle-bait ("You Won't Believe...").

**Example headline tone:**
- Good: "So what actually happens when you ask ChatGPT a question"
- Avoid: "10 SHOCKING AI Facts Big Tech Doesn't Want You To Know!!"

## 8. Application quick-reference

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Background | Near black `#14100D` |
| Body text | Off-white `#E8E2D4`, serif |
| Headings | Off-white or cyber green, monospace bold |
| Links | Cyber green `#39FF8A`, underline on hover |
| Primary CTA button | Rust `#E8672C` fill, near-black text |
| Secondary button | Outline in khaki grey, off-white text |
| Dividers | 1px khaki grey, dashed for lists / solid for section breaks |
| Category tags | Monospace, small caps avoided — sentence case, khaki grey text |
